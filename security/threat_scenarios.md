# VoiceShield AI - Voice Cloning Threat Scenarios

## Purpose

This document identifies realistic cybersecurity attacks where synthetic voice technology is used to impersonate trusted people. Voice analysis must be considered together with caller and request context.

## 1. CEO/CXO Impersonation

An attacker clones an executive's voice, creates urgency, and requests a financial transfer from an employee or finance department.

**Risk:** CRITICAL

**Action:** Block or escalate. Verify the executive through an independent trusted channel.

## 2. Government Official Impersonation

An attacker impersonates a government official and creates fear or urgency while requesting sensitive information or action.

**Risk:** HIGH / CRITICAL

**Action:** Do not provide sensitive information. Independently verify the caller.

## 3. Bank or Customer Support Impersonation

An attacker impersonates bank support and requests an OTP, PIN, password, account credential, or bank detail.

**Risk:** CRITICAL

**Action:** Never disclose credentials or OTPs. Use the organization's official contact channel.

## 4. Family Member Impersonation

An attacker clones a family member's voice, claims an emergency, and requests money or confidential information.

**Risk:** HIGH / CRITICAL

**Action:** Contact the family member through an independent known channel.

## 5. Employee or Colleague Impersonation

An attacker clones a colleague's voice and requests internal documents, passwords, account information, or company information.

**Risk:** HIGH

**Action:** Verify the colleague independently before sharing information.

## 6. Financial Fraud

Synthetic voice, an unknown caller, trusted-person impersonation, a large transaction, and urgency together indicate likely financial fraud.

**Risk:** CRITICAL

**Action:** Do not authorize the transaction. Perform independent verification.

## 7. OTP Attack

An attacker impersonates a bank employee or trusted service representative and requests an OTP.

**Risk:** CRITICAL

**Action:** Never disclose an OTP to a caller.

## Security Principle

Voice authenticity alone must not determine whether an interaction is safe. The decision must combine voice analysis, caller identity, claimed identity, request type, urgency, sensitive actions, and transaction context.
